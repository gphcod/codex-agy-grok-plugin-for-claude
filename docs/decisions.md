## 2026-10-31 S2.9: ownership evidence and request binding

Codex's confirmation round reproduced two defects in the S2.8 resolutions; both rulings amend A7.4/A7.5 in PLAN.md and the S2 freeze.

- **Process-group ownership after leader loss (A7.5).** S2.8 adopted a pgid member when no live process held the leader's pid with another creation time. Codex's counterexample: pid 700 reused by a new leader that spawns 701 and exits; 701 has pgid 700, is born after the recorded start, and no conflicting leader remains, so it was adopted and SIGKILLed. Absence of a conflicting leader is not evidence. Now: the lifecycle persists the identities of the owned descendants (pid + creation time) in `job.execution` at every heartbeat (`hooks.beatMs`, default the 10 s `BEAT_MS`; one process-table query per heartbeat while a turn runs, inside A1's allowance) and at every stop snapshot. While the recorded leader is alive with its recorded creation time, group members born at or after it are owned; once it is gone, a member is owned only if it matches a persisted identity. Other members are never signalled: `ownedExecution` returns `unknown` with `unverified` rows and an error naming their pids, so the post-exit scan leaves cleanup pending and `cancel` reports `unknown` with that reason and keeps the claim. A live leader with another creation time owns a reused group, which is neither adopted nor reported.
- **Request binding across providers (A7.4).** S2.8 looked up existing bindings per provider, so two identical calls could both find none, resolve `--last` to different providers and reserve two jobs (reproduced: two spawns under `null` and `null2`). Now the entry takes one provider-neutral binding, `claims/requests/scope-<hash of session, realpath(cwd), request id>.json`, with an exclusive create before resolving anything; it stores the fingerprint, the caller's identity and, once resolved, the provider and job id. A second caller waits on it: a different fingerprint exits 2, a bound job is replayed, a binding whose caller is proven gone before binding a job exits 2 with "a new request id is safe", and a released binding is claimed again. Any failure before the tombstone releases both the job claim and the binding. The entry's `hooks.beforeResolve` is a test seam for the delayed-resolution race.

## 2026-10-31 S2.10: close ownership and reservation failure paths

- **Missing leader record (A7.5).** The leader's identity is recorded at the spawn by a scoped query that can fail (`job.leader: null`). Without it, any live member of the child's process group is now `unverified` (no signal, claim kept, cleanup unconfirmed), never `gone`. Only a live leader whose recorded identity differs (a provably reused group) is ignored. A group member older than a verified leader is likewise unverified rather than adopted.
- **Reservation creation (A7.4).** `claimScope` writes the binding to a private temporary file and hard-links it to its name (exclusive, EEXIST when held), so a binding appears complete or not at all; a failed write or link removes the temporary file and leaves no empty binding for identical retries to wait on. The call exits 1 and an identical retry runs. Because bindings are created complete, an unreadable one was damaged afterwards; its creator cannot be proven gone, so readers wait on it until their deadline. `hooks.scopeIo` is the test seam for injected file-system failures.
